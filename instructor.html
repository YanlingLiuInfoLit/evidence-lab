Four investigation levels

1 · Guided

Cases 1–4

Students learn the investigation loop with relatively clean evidence: verify existence, compare claims with sources, evaluate AI output, and calibrate uncertainty.

Core question: What should I check?

2 · Networked

Cases 5–12

Students trace information through people, platforms, summaries, reposts, corrections, and access systems.

Core question: Where did this information actually come from?

3 · Contextual

Cases 13–28

Students weigh provenance, visual context, authority, currency, health, money, careers, and consequences.

Core question: What evidence matters for this decision?

4 · Synthesis

Cases 29–36

Students combine data, sampling, breaking-news uncertainty, source independence, translation, AI citation tracing, and bounded synthesis.

Core question: What is the strongest claim the evidence warrants?

Learning outcomes

  1. Verify provenance and existence.
  2. Select evidence strategically.
  3. Evaluate claim–evidence fit.
  4. Trace information networks and source dependence.
  5. Evaluate authority contextually.
  6. Interpret visual, quantitative, and multimodal evidence.
  7. Calibrate uncertainty.
  8. Update conclusions responsibly.
  9. Make consequential information decisions.
  10. Communicate bounded conclusions.

How to interpret the score

4

Decision

Does the conclusion fit what the evidence warrants?

3

Evidence Quality

Did the student find evidence capable of resolving the question?

2

Reasoning

Does the evidence identified as most important actually support the conclusion?

Efficiency — 1 point: Did the student stop after sufficient high-value evidence rather than checking indiscriminately? Confidence is reflective and not scored.

Evidence Lab scores are best interpreted formatively alongside investigation paths and discussion. They are not a validated standardized measure of information-literacy ability.
