# Evidence Lab

**Follow the evidence. Make the call.**

Evidence Lab is an institution-neutral information-literacy investigation game for college students, created by **Yanling Liu**, Coordinator of Information Literacy and Research Services at Indiana University Kokomo.

**Play Evidence Lab:** https://yanlingliuinfolit.github.io/evidence-lab/

## What students do

**Encounter → Investigate → Decide → Explain → Feedback → Review**

Students choose evidence, decide when they have enough, make an evidence-bounded decision, identify which evidence mattered most, reflect on confidence, and receive feedback.

## The 36-case bank

Evidence Lab contains 36 investigations across four levels:

- **Guided (Cases 1–4):** verification and uncertainty calibration
- **Networked (Cases 5–12):** source tracing and information chains
- **Contextual (Cases 13–28):** provenance, authority, currency, stakes, visual evidence, careers, money, and institutional information
- **Synthesis (Cases 29–36):** data, news, translation, AI citation tracing, and bounded synthesis

Case 36 is the Capstone finale.

## Scoring

Each investigation is worth 10 Investigator Points:

- Decision — 4
- Evidence Quality — 3
- Reasoning — 2
- Efficiency — 1

Confidence is reflective and is not scored.

## Institution-neutral design

Evidence Lab works without local customization. Fictional institutional contexts are used where needed, and library/institutional systems are described by function rather than requiring prior knowledge of a local product.

## Assessment note

The public GitHub Pages edition is intended for instruction, practice, discussion, and formative assessment. It should not be treated as a secure high-stakes examination.

## Accessibility

Essential reasoning should not depend on color, image recognition, hearing, or knowledge of a language other than the instructional language. Production use should preserve keyboard access, visible focus, text alternatives, transcripts/timelines, chart tables, and meaningful multilingual equivalents.
