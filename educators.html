Ready-to-use teaching plans

15-minute Evidence Sprint

2 min: Tell students they do not need to open everything. 8 min: Play 2–3 selected cases. 5 min: Debrief: Which evidence changed your decision? When did you have enough?

Suggested starter set: Cases 1, 8, and 29.

Asynchronous assignment

Assign a Practice session before class. Reflection prompts: (1) Where did your first impression change? (2) Which evidence was strongest and why? (3) What added little? (4) Where does the same reasoning appear in this course?

Open student game

Four levels

Guided: verify and calibrate. Networked: trace sources and transformations. Contextual: weigh provenance, authority, currency, and stakes. Synthesis: combine multiple moves and communicate a bounded conclusion.

What the score means

Decision 4 · Evidence Quality 3 · Reasoning 2 · Efficiency 1. Confidence is reflective only. Scores are best used formatively alongside investigation paths and discussion; Evidence Lab is not a validated standardized test of information literacy.

Course applications

Composition · First-year seminars · Research methods · AI literacy · Journalism/media literacy · Career preparation · General education · Disciplinary source evaluation.
